zipline

From "this is amazing" to "this actually works", without the crash in between.

Most people who start working with an assistant hit the same curve. It's exciting, then it confidently gets something wrong, goes round in circles on the twentieth version of the same email or script, or can't make what they had in mind. Many give up there. zipline is a set of working habits for Claude Code that takes you straight across: you steer, the assistant drafts, and built-in checks stop the two of you going in circles.

This is not an image generator. It won't make AI art. Graphics are built the way a designer builds them, from real fonts, layouts and shapes, like the curve above.

Set it up for your work

Answer a few questions and this page writes a file called workalong.md. Put it in the top folder of your zipline copy and the assistant works the way it says. Your answers never leave this page. Nothing is sent anywhere; the file is made right here in your browser.

The kind of work

Each kind gets its own working rules and tools, on top of the ones they all share.

What's happened so far

These ask what has happened, not how good you are with assistants. Almost everyone rates themselves highly, so that question tells us nothing.

Has one been confidently wrong in a way that cost you time or money?

Is there a job it now does for you regularly that you trust?

Have you typed a command into a terminal?

Your setup

Who reviews the work before it's merged?

With two or more, nothing is committed or pushed to main from the assistant's side: work goes on a branch and through a pull request.

Who will use this?

What kind of computer is it running on?

On a managed computer, anything that goes out over the network needs your OK unless it's on this list.

What will the assistant be able to get at?

What needs your OK

These always need your OK, at every level: sending anything, deleting your files, spending money, installing software, and prices, dates and promises to customers. For all but the last, the action stops until you type a short phrase like email james; "yes" doesn't count. A price in a draft can't be spotted automatically, so for that one the assistant asks. You can add to this list, not take away.
